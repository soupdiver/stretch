Reading at human speed.

A short essay about attention, interfaces, and keeping the details within reach.

Attention is a limited resource, but the web rarely behaves as though it is. A useful article can arrive surrounded by long introductions, repeated explanations, and details that matter only when you need them. The answer is not to throw that information away. It is to make the important ideas easy to find while keeping the complete source available whenever you want to look more closely.

Good interfaces make room for different depths of reading. On a first pass, a reader may want the main point; on a second pass, the qualifications and examples become important. Shorter should not mean less trustworthy. An interface that lets readers move between these levels can respect both their time and their curiosity, without replacing a thoughtful explanation with a misleading slogan.

The details still matter. A deadline, an exception, or a carefully worded condition can change what a paragraph means. Any tool that shortens prose should preserve those distinctions and make the original easy to recover. Read the compact version to get oriented, then open the source whenever the stakes are high. The goal is not to stop reading; it is to choose where your attention goes.
