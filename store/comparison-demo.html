Reading a long page.

Attention is limited. Long introductions and repeated explanations can make a useful article harder to read. Show the main ideas first, but keep the complete source available for anyone who wants the details.

Good interfaces let readers choose how much detail they need. A quick first pass might be enough, while a second reading may need every example and qualification. Shorter text should not mean less trustworthy text.

The details still matter. A condition or exception can change the meaning. Whenever the stakes are high, open the original and check what it actually says.
